Frequently Asked Questions

What is fundamental analysis?
Fundamental analysis is a method of evaluating a security to measure its intrinsic value, by examining related economic and financial factors. Analysts study everything that can affect the security's value, from macroeconomic factors like the state of the economy and industry conditions to microeconomic factors like the effectiveness of the company's management.
What are the most important financial statements?
The three most important financial statements are the Income Statement (showing profitability over a period), the Balance Sheet (showing assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific point in time), and the Cash Flow Statement (showing the actual cash moving in and out of the business).
How is a P/E ratio calculated?
The Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio is calculated by dividing a company's current stock price by its earnings per share (EPS). It is a widely used valuation metric that helps investors determine how much they are paying for $1 of the company's earnings.
What is free cash flow (FCF)?
Free cash flow (FCF) represents the cash a company generates after accounting for cash outflows to support operations and maintain its capital assets. It's an important metric because it shows the actual cash available for dividends, debt reduction, or reinvestment.
Why are dividends important to value investors?
Dividends provide a tangible, regular return on investment regardless of the stock price fluctuations. For value investors, a consistent and growing dividend often signals financial health, disciplined management, and confidence in future earnings.
