Methodology & Formulas

This calculator computes standard bond metrics using standard financial mathematics. It assumes a flat yield curve and reinvestment of coupons at the calculated YTM.

Yield to Maturity (YTM)

YTM is the internal rate of return (IRR) of the bond's cash flows. Because the formula for YTM cannot be solved algebraically for bonds with more than one period, this calculator uses the Newton-Raphson numerical method to find the precise yield where the present value of future cash flows equals the current market price.

Current Yield

Calculated as the annual coupon payment divided by the current market price. It ignores the capital gain or loss realized at maturity.

Duration Metrics

Macaulay Duration is the weighted average time until all cash flows are received, measured in years. The weights are the present values of each cash flow as a percentage of the total bond price.

Modified Duration adjusts Macaulay duration to estimate price sensitivity. The formula is: Modified Duration = Macaulay Duration / (1 + (YTM / Frequency)). It estimates the percentage drop in bond price for a 1% increase in interest rates.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between Current Yield and Yield to Maturity?
Current yield only looks at the coupon payment relative to today's price. YTM factors in the coupon payments, the reinvestment of those payments, and any capital gain or loss if the bond was bought at a discount or premium to its par value, assuming it is held to maturity.
Why does the bond price go down when yield goes up?
Bond prices and yields have an inverse relationship. If newly issued bonds pay a higher yield, older bonds with lower coupon rates become less attractive. Their price must drop so their overall return (YTM) matches the new, higher market rates. You can visualize this in the "Price Sensitivity" tab.
How is Modified Duration useful?
It acts as a risk measure. If a bond has a modified duration of 5.0, a 1% increase in interest rates will cause the bond's price to drop by roughly 5%. Higher duration means higher interest rate risk.
