Understanding Position Sizing & Risk Management

Position sizing is a fundamental concept in trading and investing. It refers to determining the appropriate number of shares or contracts to purchase based on your total account value, your predefined risk tolerance, and the specific parameters of a trade (entry price and stop loss).

A common rule of thumb utilized by professional traders is to risk no more than 1% to 2% of total account capital on any single trade. This mathematical approach helps protect against a string of consecutive losses, preserving capital for future opportunities.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How does the Position Size Calculator work?

The calculator determines your exact position size by taking your total dollar risk (Account Size × Risk %) and dividing it by the risk per share (Entry Price - Stop Loss Price). This ensures that if your stop loss is triggered, you will only lose the predefined dollar amount.

What is Risk/Reward Ratio?

The risk/reward ratio compares the potential profit of a trade to its potential loss. For example, a 1:2 risk/reward ratio means you are risking $1 to potentially make $2. Entering an optional Take Profit target in our calculator will automatically display your R/R ratio and visual metrics.

Should I go long or short?

The calculator automatically detects whether you are calculating a long or short position based on your entry and stop loss prices. If your entry is higher than your stop loss, it assumes a long position. If your entry is lower than your stop loss, it calculates for a short position.

Methodology

The mathematical formulas used in this calculator are:

  • Dollar Risk: Total Account Size × (Risk % / 100)
  • Risk Per Share: Absolute Value of (Entry Price - Stop Loss Price)
  • Position Size (Shares): Floor(Dollar Risk / Risk Per Share)
  • Total Cost: Position Size × Entry Price
  • Reward Ratio: (Take Profit - Entry Price) / Risk Per Share
