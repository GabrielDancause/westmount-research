Methodology

The Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) model is a valuation method used to estimate the value of an investment based on its expected future cash flows. It rests on the principle that the value of a company today is the sum of all the cash it will generate in the future, discounted back to today's dollars.

  • Free Cash Flow Projections: This calculator assumes the current Free Cash Flow grows at the specified Growth Rate for the next 10 years.
  • Discounting: Each future cash flow is discounted back to its present value using the Discount Rate, which typically represents the Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) or an investor's required rate of return.
  • Terminal Value: Beyond the 10-year projection period, the model calculates a Terminal Value using the Gordon Growth Model, assuming cash flows grow at a constant, perpetual Terminal Growth Rate. This terminal value is also discounted back to the present.
  • Intrinsic Value Per Share: The Present Value of the 10-year FCFs and the Present Value of the Terminal Value are summed to get the Total Intrinsic Value of the company. This total is then divided by the number of Shares Outstanding to arrive at the Intrinsic Value Per Share.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I use for the Discount Rate?

The discount rate is often a company's Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC), which is the blended cost of its debt and equity. Alternatively, many individual investors use their own "required rate of return," such as 9% or 10%, which represents the long-term historical average return of the stock market.

What is a realistic Terminal Growth Rate?

The terminal growth rate assumes the company will grow at that rate forever. Because no company can grow faster than the overall economy indefinitely, a rate between 2% and 3% is standard, as it aligns with long-term historical GDP growth and inflation.

Why project for 10 years?

A 5 to 10-year projection period is typical in DCF models. It is long enough to capture an expected high-growth or transitional phase before the company matures and settles into a stable, perpetual growth rate modeled by the terminal value.
