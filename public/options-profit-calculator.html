Methodology

The Profit/Loss chart is generated by evaluating the option payoff function at expiration across a range of underlying asset prices. The x-axis represents the stock price at expiration, and the y-axis represents the total dollar profit or loss. For strategies involving stock (Covered Call, Protective Put), the initial stock purchase price is factored into the total cost basis.

The Options Greeks (Delta, Gamma, Theta, Vega) are estimated using the standard Black-Scholes-Merton model for European options. The calculation assumes continuous dividend yield of 0% and a constant risk-free interest rate of 5%. Theta is expressed as a 1-day decay. Note that these are theoretical values and actual market prices/greeks may differ due to volatility skew, early exercise risk (for American options), and market bid/ask spreads.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the breakeven point mean?

The breakeven point is the exact stock price at expiration where your strategy makes $0 profit and $0 loss. It accounts for the premiums paid or collected.

Why is my Max Profit shown as Infinite?

For strategies like a Long Call or a Long Put (technically bounded by the stock going to zero, but practically very high), the profit potential is theoretically unlimited if the underlying stock price continues to rise indefinitely.

What is Delta?

Delta measures the expected change in the option's price for a $1 change in the underlying stock. A Delta of 0.50 means the option price should increase by $0.50 if the stock goes up by $1.
